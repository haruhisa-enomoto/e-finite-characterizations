# How Lean proves the E-finite characterizations

Let Λ be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field k. A two-term presilting complex U has no positive self-extensions in the homotopy category. The nonnegative real combinations of the projective classes of its indecomposable summands form its cone. The algebra is **E-finite** if the union of these cones contains every integral class in K₀(proj Λ). Lean works with finite right Λ-modules by taking left modules over the opposite ring. The November 25 manuscript groups the characterizations into 17 tests over any field, two further tests over an infinite field, and three geometric tests over an algebraically closed field. Each test is proved equivalent to E-finiteness of the same algebra. [Manuscript](../MANUSCRIPT.md) · [Lean statement and assembly](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Characterizations/Equivalences.lean)

Two constructions explain most of the implications. At a **covered** weight, perpendicular reduction gives finitely many stable simples with independent classes. At a **missing** integral class, a family of projective differentials produces too many stable bricks for those finiteness and independence properties to hold. A brick is a module whose endomorphism ring is a division ring. A semibrick is a set of pairwise Hom-orthogonal bricks up to isomorphism. The routes below explain how these constructions enter the individual tests. The concordance locates all 44 theorem, proposition, lemma and corollary occurrences, including introductory restatements. ## Covered weights and perpendicular reduction

For a differential P⁻¹ → P⁰ with cokernel C and Nakayama kernel N (the kernel after applying the Nakayama functor), its Euler weight on X is dimₖ Hom(C,X) − dimₖ Hom(X,N). If U is presilting, the semistable category at a weight in the relative interior of its cone is J(U), the modules X for which both Hom spaces vanish. Strict and weak inequalities on subobjects and quotients similarly identify the two numerical torsion pairs. Individual stable simples can be separated by projective weights, giving linear independence of their classes. [Cone interiors and torsion pairs](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Stability/ConeTorsionPairs.lean) · [Separating weights](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Stability/StableSeparationWeights.lean)

For the reduction algebra, let B be the cokernel of the basic Bongartz completion of U. Divide B by the trace of C, the sum of the images of all maps C → B. The quotient G is a projective generator of J(U). The induced map End(B) → End(G) is onto, and its kernel consists precisely of maps through finite sums of C. Thus Hom(G,−) gives the exact equivalence with finite right modules over the specified quotient End(B)/[C]. [Basic completion](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Silting/Reduction/BasicBongartzCompletion.lean) · [Endomorphism quotient and equivalence](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Silting/Reduction/PerpendicularReduction.lean)

The paper counts the complementary projective covers. Lean proves two inequalities. Write m for the number of basic summands of U, s for the number of simples of J(U), and n for the ambient simple count. The m summand classes vanish on every stable simple. One further weight per simple, positive on that simple and zero on the others, gives m + s independent classes. Conversely, completing U at each ambient projective shows that m + s weights suffice to span all projective classes. Two indecomposable cokernel summands mapping nontrivially to the same stable simple are isomorphic. Those with no such map contribute classes already in the span of U. Hence m + s = n over every field. [Spanning argument](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Silting/Reduction/BongartzCounting.lean) · [Simple count](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Silting/Reduction/PerpendicularSimpleCount.lean)

## From a missing class to stable bricks

Choose projective terms representing a missing class γ. For every differential p on these terms there is a nonzero map coker p → ker(νp). Choose such a map of smallest image dimension. A noninvertible nonzero endomorphism of its image B would give a smaller image, so B is a brick. The same minimality controls its proper submodules and makes B stable at the corrected integral weight (dimₖ B)γ − γ(B)[Λ]. Only finitely many corrected weights can occur for the chosen terms. For each fixed brick, a nonzero minor bounds the locus of differentials at which it can occur. Over an infinite field finitely many such loci cannot exhaust the presentation space. This yields infinitely many stable bricks of one weight and one ordinary class. [Missing-class witnesses](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Stability/MissingWeightWitnesses.lean)

Over a finite field, Lean counts over extensions and returns to the ground field. For k of order q and every extension degree s, the resulting lower bound is qˢ/(c₀s) across a fixed finite list of weights, at bounded endolength and ground dimension at most s times a fixed constant. Endolength measures length over the endomorphism ring. Frobenius descent identifies each ground stable with one orbit of geometric stable constituents. Their number is the degree of the ground endomorphism field, and each has dimension equal to the ground stable's endolength. These arguments give infinitely many ground bricks at one weight and endolength, and a pair of distinct stable bricks at one weight and ordinary class. The ordinary class of the entire infinite family can vary over a finite field. [Finite-field bound](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/ScalarExtension/SharpFiniteFieldCount.lean) · [Absolute Frobenius correspondence](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/ScalarExtension/AbsoluteFrobeniusCorrespondence.lean)

## The arbitrary-field tests in Theorem A(I)

**Equal-class Hom, numerical disjointness, and semibricks.** A vanishing Hom space between modules of the same nonzero class yields a finite semibrick jointly stable at one weight with dependent rational classes. Coverage contradicts the independence just proved. In the reverse direction, two distinct stable bricks of one weight and class have zero Hom. A torsion class and its right orthogonal supply the Hom vanishing in the numerical-disjointness test. Under equal-class Hom, each finite semibrick admits a common strict stability weight and has independent classes. Consequently semibricks have at most n elements. At size n the strict weight annihilates a basis and is zero, whose stable objects are the ordinary simples. A missing class gives an infinite semibrick and hence also an n-element counterexample to uniqueness. [Extraction](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Stability/RationalExtraction.lean) · [Core equivalences](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Characterizations/HomCharacterizations.lean)

**Compact torsion classes and fixed real weights.** For a torsion class generated by one finite module, separation of quotient and submodule classes gives weights testing the two sides of the torsion pair. Under coverage the corresponding presilting differentials give finite approximations. The stronger body theorem fixes a real weight θ and proves all seven conditions equivalent, even for an E-infinite algebra. Its crucial step is simultaneous rank control. On a rational ray in a torsion chamber one differential of a positive multiple of the class gives a surjective Hom map into a finite generating witness and an injective one into a finite cogenerating witness. Lean obtains both ranks at one point over an algebraic closure and descends them together by the regular representation of a finite field extension. The resulting differential is presilting. Cone saturation and closure give the criterion for real θ. [Compact torsion equivalence](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Subcategories/CompactTorsionEquivalences.lean) · [Simultaneous rank tests](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Stability/OneSidedDeterminants.lean) · [Fixed-weight theorem](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Stability/ChamberRankTests.lean)

**The torsion-class Grothendieck monoid.** The revised test concerns the canonical map from the monoid of a torsion class, with relations from its inherited short exact sequences, to ambient K₀. Equal-class Hom provides nonzero maps between modules of equal class. Pushing out a short exact sequence along such a map reduces the comparison to a proper quotient of the module chosen to represent the kernel class. The quotient and extension closure of the torsion class keeps the resulting relations inside its monoid. Induction identifies the two monoid classes. For the converse it suffices to test the weak numerical torsion classes at integral weights, where the stable witnesses detect failure of injectivity. The separate group-completion result identifies the image with the subgroup supported on the composition factors occurring in the class. Injectivity before group completion is the characterization in A(I). [Monoid equivalence](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/GrothendieckGroups/TorsionMonoidEmbedding.lean) · [Group comparison](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/GrothendieckGroups/TorsionGrothendieckGroups.lean)

**Wide subcategories, one-brick filtrations, and brick labels.** The simples of a wide subcategory form a semibrick. In the E-finite case they admit a common stabilizing weight. Maximizing the dimension of a presilting cone stabilizing them excludes every additional stable simple and realizes the wide subcategory as J(U), which is functorially finite. For a single brick B one can choose a facet at which B is the unique stable simple. The semistable category there is Filt(B), and the strict and weak torsion classes give a cover T′ ⊊ T with Filt(B) = T ∩ (T′)⊥. Conversely such an interval gives a left approximation for Filt(B). A missing class supplies a brick for which this approximation fails. [Wide perpendiculars](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Subcategories/WidePerpendicular.lean) · [Cover and interval equality](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Subcategories/BrickLabels.lean) · [One-brick obstruction](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Subcategories/FieldWideWitness.lean)

**Endolength and polynomial growth.** A brick's submodule polytope records the convex hull of its submodule classes. After normalization by the dimension of the brick's endomorphism division ring, each vertex lies on a finite grid controlled by the endolength. Stability separates the polytopes of distinct bricks under E-finiteness, giving finiteness at each endolength and hence the corresponding stable-brick test. For the stronger polynomial bound in composition length, Lean counts realizable sign patterns of submodule weights. The missing-class families above contradict the endolength tests, and their growth contradicts the polynomial bound. [Normalized polytopes](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Stability/NormalizedPolytopes.lean) · [Polynomial growth bound](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Stability/SharpBrickGrowth.lean)

**Generic bricks and generic grains.** To exclude a generic brick under E-finiteness, Lean finds a division-ring semistability weight. Its presilting representative allows a reduction to an E-finite algebra with fewer simples, where the same exclusion follows by induction. A generic grain is an infinite-dimensional indecomposable endofinite module N with Ext¹(X,N) = 0 for finite modules X cogenerated by N. Its endomorphism ring is local, and the socle of N over that ring is a generic brick. For the converse the library has two constructions. A family of stable representations produces an infinite-dimensional simple Artinian epimorphic target whose simple module is a generic brick. The manuscript constructs a grain using Cohn’s universal division ring. Lean builds a division ring using well-ordered series on a biordered free group and uses its noncommuting coefficients in a generic differential of injectives. The endofinite kernel realizes the numerical torsion pair. At **each prescribed missing integral weight**, the fixed-weight criterion rules out a finite cogenerator, and the kernel has a generic-grain direct summand. [Exclusion](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/LargeModules/GenericBrickExclusion.lean) · [Grain equivalence](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/LargeModules/GenericGrainCriterion.lean) · [Prescribed-weight conclusion](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Stability/ChamberRankTests.lean)

**Thick generation.** Inside the bounded homotopy category of finite projectives, take a summand-closed two-term subcategory H satisfying two-out-of-three for triangles of two-term objects. Choose a basic presilting U in H with a maximum number of indecomposable summands. Block extensions reduce shifted Hom dimensions. The rigid pencil and the splitting of a nilpotent component show that the remaining objects orthogonal to U in both directions belong to add(U). Thus H is exactly the two-term part of thick(U). For the converse, thick generation would give each differential a presilting complex with the same inversion tests. Its singleton universal localization is finite-dimensional. At a missing class, a stable-family chart forces an infinite-dimensional localization, a contradiction. [Bounded-projective theorem](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Silting/ThickSubcategories/BoundedProjectiveHomotopy.lean) · [Converse by inversion](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Silting/ThickSubcategories/ThickGenerationCriterion.lean) · [Maximal presilting complexes](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Silting/ThickSubcategories/MaximalPresilting.lean)

**Universal localization and simple Artinian targets.** The paper obtains finite localizations from thick generation. Lean also proves finiteness directly by induction on the simple count. Choose one noninvertible differential σ from the family. For nonzero class, comparison with a rigid differential on stabilized projective terms shows that inversion of σ forces inversion of the rigid differential on every module, including infinite-dimensional modules. For class zero, Fitting decomposition reduces inversion to annihilating a projective summand. In both cases the problem passes to an E-finite algebra of smaller rank. Inducing the entire family there proves finiteness for arbitrary sets of maps. In the other direction one missing class gives a differential with an infinite-dimensional localization even after quotienting by the common annihilator of its finite-dimensional modules. The simple Artinian target test also follows from the generic-brick constructions. ## Infinite and algebraically closed fields

In A(II) the two tests fix an ordinary composition vector, for all bricks or for stable bricks at each integral weight. Under E-finiteness this finiteness follows from the bounded endolength at fixed vector. Over an infinite field a missing class gives infinitely many stable bricks at **one** vector and weight by the minor argument. The field assumption enters this reverse implication. [Fixed-class finiteness](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Characterizations/FixedClassFiniteness.lean)

In A(III) the representation varieties come from a Morita-equivalent basic algebra. Under E-finiteness, independence of stable classes determines the multiplicities in the polystable representative of a semistable module of fixed class. The paper uses the description of King closed points by polystables. Lean splits extensions while preserving evaluation in the projective moduli space. Conversely two distinct stable modules of one weight and vector invert a common differential. A trace on its determinant chart separates their closed points. For the other two tests, Hom vanishing defines open loci, and perpendicular reduction describes membership in a wide subcategory. The resulting characterizations are openness of every brick orbit and openness of every wide locus. Weyman's corollary separately identifies brick-discreteness with multiplicity-freeness on each irreducible component in arbitrary characteristic. [Geometric equivalences](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Geometry/GeometricCriteria.lean) · [King moduli](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Geometry/KingModuli.lean) · [Component semi-invariants](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Geometry/ComponentMultiplicity.lean)

## Rank at most two

Theorem B identifies E-finiteness with both τ-tilting finiteness and brick-finiteness over every field when n ≤ 2. For two presilting boundary classes E and L with Hom(E,L[1]) = 0, the integer dimₖ Hom(L,E[1]) measures the depth of the subdivision. Inserting the sum [E] + [L] and comparing with a nonsplit block extension decreases this integer in each child sector. Iteration gives finitely many compatible sectors. In rank two the two mixed quadrants suffice together with the same-sign cones. The paper reads τ-tilting finiteness from the finite fan and invokes the general brick-finiteness theorem. Lean uses the finite sector cover to bound the indecomposable presilting cokernels and to produce finitely many weights testing all bricks. Conversely τ-tilting finiteness bounds the rays, and compatible completions cover the plane. A missing class already forces infinitely many bricks over any field. The argument includes ranks zero and one. [Sector subdivision](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Silting/Fans/SectorSubdivision.lean) · [Brick-finiteness](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Silting/Fans/RankTwoBricks.lean) · [Theorem B](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Silting/Fans/RankTwoTauTilting.lean)

## Further consequences in the body

**Exact embeddings and torsion radicals.** An exact functor preserves equality of Grothendieck classes. Faithfulness reflects zero objects and fullness lifts a nonzero morphism, so the equal-class Hom criterion descends along every exact fully faithful functor. Restriction along a surjective algebra map gives the quotient case. For Lemma 7.2, take the direct sum of the specified torsion subobjects. Its generated torsion class lies inside the original class, is functorially finite, and has the same torsion decompositions on the specified objects. [Exact embeddings](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Subcategories/ExactRestriction.lean) · [Torsion radicals](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Subcategories/TorsionRadical.lean)

**Cancellation.** The group completion of the exact monoid of a torsion class embeds in the ambient Grothendieck group. Thus the dimension map is injective precisely when the monoid is cancellative. An exact duality sends the relation of a short exact sequence to the same relation with its outer terms exchanged. Applying the inverse duality recovers the original monoid classes. The equivalence for torsion-free classes follows by passing to the opposite algebra. [Monoid cancellation](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/GrothendieckGroups/MonoidCancellation.lean)

**Ground-field dimension.** Finitely many composition vectors occur in a bounded ground-field dimension. If infinitely many bricks have one such dimension, the algebra is E-infinite. Over an infinite field its stable witnesses give one integral weight and one positive ground-field dimension containing infinitely many isomorphism classes. [Dimension and stability](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/Characterizations/BrickDimension.lean)

**Artin targets.** To compute first Tor, take the free presentation of the restricted regular module of the target ring. Push out its first syzygy inclusion along the unit of extension of scalars. For a universal localization the two end terms of the resulting extension invert the designated projective maps, hence its middle term carries the localization action. The induced syzygy map factors the injective pushout inclusion after extension by adjunction, giving zero first Tor. If the target is Artinian, the descending chain condition makes its finite-dimensional residual the kernel of one finite-dimensional representation. The infinite-dimensional residual quotient supplied by a missing weight rules this out. Theorem B gives the rank-two conclusion. [First Tor](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/RingEpimorphisms/LocalizationTor.lean) · [Artinian quotient](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/RingEpimorphisms/ArtinianResidual.lean) · [Corollary 7.9](../../EFiniteCharacterizations/RingEpimorphisms/ArtinTargets.lean)
